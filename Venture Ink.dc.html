The new generation of Indian VCs is betting on a different future

Smaller funds, sharper theses and a willingness to be early — the country's next investment cycle is being written by people who did not run the last one.

By Venture Ink September 2, 2026 7 min read

Fig. 1 — Median close size, new India-focused funds

2019 $142M
2021 $210M
2023 $96M
2026 $68M

Fund closes tracked by Venture Ink. Figures are self-reported.

For most of the last decade, Indian venture capital ran on a simple trade: buy growth early in a market that was getting online for the first time, and let scale sort out the economics later. That trade is over. The funds raising money in 2026 are smaller, more concentrated and far more explicit about where their edge comes from.

Nine of the fourteen new India-focused funds closed in the past eighteen months came in under $75 million. Almost all of them describe themselves as thesis-led rather than stage-led — a distinction that would have sounded precious three years ago and now reads as risk management.

The end of the index approach

The old model rewarded coverage. Write enough cheques into enough categories and one outcome pays for the fund. What has changed is not conviction but cost: seed valuations held while exit paths lengthened, which quietly removed the margin that made the index approach work.

“We are not trying to see every deal anymore. We are trying to be the obvious first call in four categories.”

Partner at a 2025-vintage seed fund

That concentration shows up in portfolio construction. Where a 2021 seed fund might have held sixty positions, the current cohort is building books of eighteen to twenty-five, with half the capital reserved for follow-on.

What founders should read into it

For founders, the practical effect is a narrower window and a harder question. Term sheets are arriving faster for companies inside a fund's declared thesis and much slower for everything else. The diligence has shifted from market size to whether the team has an unfair reason to be the one building this.

It is a less forgiving market and, for the operators who have been through one cycle already, a more legible one. The people writing cheques now are betting on a version of India that compounds rather than one that inflects — and they are sizing their funds accordingly.
